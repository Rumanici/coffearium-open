## What it does

After pulping, the seeds are still coated in mucilage. In fermented washed coffee, microbes loosen it in a tank over many hours. A demucilager does the same job by force: it rubs the mucilage off within about a minute, so the coffee can be dried without fermenting.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The FAO manual for Lao PDR puts it plainly: the mucilage is removed immediately after pulping and is not fermented.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

The idea is old. A mechanical demucilager built at the Hawaii Agricultural Experiment Station in the 1950s beat the mucilage off with rotating steel-rod blades while coffee and water travelled up a tube.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Colombia's research centre, Cenicafé, later built the DESLIM upflow demucilagers and combined them with water-free pulping in the Becolsub module.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

## How the machine works

The common design is a vertical cylinder with a rotor inside.

- **Feed** – a hopper delivers pulped coffee continuously to the machine.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the DESLIM, a short screw at the bottom forces the coffee upwards.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the Hawaiian prototype, coffee and water were funnelled in together through a feeder tube below the beater.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Upward flow** – the coffee is pushed upwards through the cylinder; Cenicafé calls the DESLIM an upward vertical grain flow demucilager.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Water enters at mid-height for demucilaging and at the top for washing, where grain and water flow in counter-current.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Rotor and agitators** – the rotor is the part that creates the shear on the mass of coffee needed to separate the mucilage from the seeds.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The DESLIM 1000 carries 15 agitators.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> Cenicafé gives a rotor speed of 870 rpm for its machine;<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> the Hawaiian beaters ran at 1,000 and 1,200 rpm.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Screen** – mucilage and fine material leave through the perforations of the casing.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Outlets** – clean parchment leaves at the top. In the Becolsub module, a screw conveyor mixes the concentrated mucilage with the pulp.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

## Water, energy and capacity

Water use is the main reason for the method. A conventional Colombian wet mill can use about 40 litres per kilogram of dry parchment coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Washing fermented coffee in the tank brings this down to about 4.2 L/kg, while washing and grading in open channels without recirculation uses about 39 L/kg.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> With the DESLIM, water use fell below 1.0 L/kg and could be brought down to 0.6 L/kg.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> For the DESLIM in the Becolsub module, Cenicafé specifies 0.7–1.0 L/kg.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> The FAO manual for Lao PDR gives 0.5 L per kg of fresh cherry for pulper/demucilager units.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

Cenicafé built three DESLIM sizes: 600, 1,000 and 3,000 kg of cherry per hour, with power ratings of 0.89, 1.34 and 4.92 kW.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The water flow is set to the coffee flow: for a 300 kg/h module it is 0.70–1.00 L/min, for a 2,500 kg/h module 5.83–8.33 L/min.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> The Hawaiian prototype handled the coffee from a pulper taking 3,000 lb (about 1,360 kg) of cherry per hour, with a 2 hp motor.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

## After the machine

Mechanically demucilaged coffee can be dried immediately, with no further treatment.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> If it must wait overnight – up to 14 hours – Cenicafé advises against holding it under water.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The FAO manual adds that the units remove the need for fermentation tanks and washing systems and lower the risk of over-fermentation.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

The same machines appear in other processes. The FAO manual calls pulp-and-demucilage processing "semi-washed".<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The term is not used consistently. The Codex code of practice uses "semi-washed" for the opposite case: green coffee from pulped beans dried within their mucilaginous parchment.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> And an importer lists "semi-washed" as another name for wet-hulled coffee (*giling basah*), a different process in which the parchment is removed before the bean is dry.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> This entry uses "semi-washed" only in the FAO sense. Where only part of the mucilage is taken off, the coffee moves towards the honey and pulped-natural styles, which have their own entries.

**Other ways to remove mucilage without fermenting.** Enzymes are one: in a 2025 Colombian study, mucilage was removed with a commercial pectin lyase.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> Alkali is another: in Hawaii, a 6% solution broke the mucilage down in about one minute.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> The chemistry of both is covered in the mucilage entry.

## The mechanics: shear, residence time and water

**Shear.** The mucilage is a gel attached to the parchment. The rotor puts the moving mass of coffee under shear – Cenicafé speaks of "cutting stresses" – and this is what separates the mucilage from the seeds.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Cenicafé attributes the effect mainly to these shear forces, from the relative movement of fluid and grains, and not simply to friction or collisions between grains.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It observed high mucilage detachment in the first moments of the process.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the Hawaiian machine, the mucilage was beaten off by the blades while the suspension travelled up the tube.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

**Residence time.** How long a seed stays in the rotor zone sets how much mucilage comes off and how much damage is done. Seeds spent 63 s in the DESLIM 1000.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> A related Cenicafé washer for already-fermented coffee (ECOLAV) needed only 16.9 s and 7 agitators, because the mucilage had already been loosened; its specific power was 10.9% of that of the demucilager.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The authors read this as efficient use of mechanical energy once fermentation (or enzymes) has degraded the mucilage.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

**Coffee flow.** Residence time depends on the feed rate. When the flow of coffee is too high, the seeds pass through faster than recommended and come out partly demucilaged.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> When it is too low, residence time is very long and the risk of mechanical damage rises.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

**Water-to-coffee ratio.** Water carries the mucilage through the screen. Cenicafé ties water flow to coffee flow (see Reference values).<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Too much water is also a problem: with higher specific water flows, the pulp no longer retains the mucilage and contamination rises because the pulp is washed.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

## Sensory comparisons with fermented coffee

The evidence points in different directions.

- **Cenicafé, 1995–1996.** Across 77 paired cup comparisons, 127 attribute-by-attribute results were reported: 56 favoured mechanical removal, 40 showed no difference and 31 favoured fermentation.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Cenicafé concluded that mechanically demucilaged coffee is equal or superior to naturally fermented coffee in the cup.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Cenicafé, 2009.** All samples dried immediately after demucilaging scored above 5 on the scale used, against 95% of samples held dry overnight and 75% of samples held under water; no ferment or dirty defects were found.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Yunnan, 2019.** In a controlled Catimor trial, the demucilaged process gave a beverage comparable to the depulped, fermented one.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> The demucilaged beans were still held under clean water in a tank for 12 or 72 hours before washing.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> The fermentation waters of fermented coffee held about ten times more of the measured compounds than those of demucilaged coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>
- **2021, three Q graders.** Pulped coffee kept under water for 36 hours scored significantly higher in flavour, aroma, acidity, body and uniformity than mechanically processed coffee, with a noted fruity aroma.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> The fermented roasted beans held more ethanol, isoamyl alcohol, 3-methylbutanal, benzaldehyde, acetaldehyde and ethyl acetate; pyrazines, furans and other roast volatiles did not differ.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>
- **Kenya.** On a university farm in Nyeri, SL 28 and Ruiru 11 coffee was either pulped in an eco-pulper and passed through a demucilager, or pulped and fermented before washing, then dried. Final cup scores were close for all methods; the demucilaged coffee scored slightly higher for SL 28, and for Ruiru 11 the differences were not significant. The demucilaged coffee had more pyrazines, the fermented wet-pulper coffee more furans.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup>
- **Practice guidance.** The FAO manual for Lao PDR states that demucilaging adds body and character to the liquor.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The Hawaiian bulletin cites an earlier report that alkali-demucilaged coffee cupped better than fermented coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

The disagreement probably reflects what each study compared. Fermentation adds fermentation volatiles;<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> whether a panel rewards them depends on the coffee and the cup profile it is looking for. Another study used mechanical removal as a no-fermentation control and found significant differences in quality between treatments; its abstract does not say how the control ranked.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

## Risks and defects

- **Partial demucilaging.** Overfeeding shortens residence time; the coffee comes out partly demucilaged, with stained parchment and a ferment defect in the cup.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Mechanical damage.** Underfeeding lengthens residence time and raises the risk of mechanical damage.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> In Cenicafé's seed trials with the three Becolsub modules, 98.9% of demucilaged seeds remained viable and double-root damage stayed below 1%.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the Hawaiian trials no physical damage to the green bean was noticeable on close examination.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Cenicafé's washer for fermented coffee caused 0.13–0.58% mechanical damage.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Holding wet coffee.** Demucilaged coffee kept under water overnight scored worse than coffee dried at once.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Heating.** A fast rotor turns mechanical energy into heat. None of the sources consulted for this entry measured the temperature rise of the coffee in a demucilager, so no value is given here.
- **Wastewater.** The mucilage does not disappear – it leaves as a concentrated stream. In the Becolsub module it is mixed into the pulp by a screw conveyor, which holds back more than half of it; with too much water, the pulp is washed and pollution rises.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Cenicafé reports that washing fermented coffee generates 30 g of chemical oxygen demand per kg of cherry.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Open questions

- **Cup parity.** Whether mechanically demucilaged coffee matches fermented coffee depends on the study.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a>,<a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> No study consulted here compared several machines and origins with the same panel.
- **Heat and the living seed.** The temperature reached in the rotor zone, and whether it affects the seed, is not reported in the sources consulted for this entry.
- **Residual mucilage and flavour.** The link between the small residue of mucilage left (under 2%)<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> and cup flavour has not been measured in these sources.

## Key studies

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Roa et al. (Cenicafé) – Ecological processing at farm level.</strong> Describes the Becolsub system: dry pulping, an upflow demucilager and mixing of mucilage into the pulp. Reports water use below 1 L per kg of dry parchment with the DESLIM, and cup comparisons whose attribute results mostly favoured mechanical removal.</p>
<p class="meta">Cenicafé technical report · <a href="https://www.ctahr.hawaii.edu/hawaii/downloads/Low_Water_use_processing.pdf">PDF</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Pabón, Sanz & Oliveros, 2009 – Handling demucilaged coffee.</strong> Tested what to do with coffee after the DESLIM. Coffee dried immediately cupped best; holding it overnight under water lowered quality, and feeding the machine too fast or too slowly caused incomplete removal or damage.</p>
<p class="meta">Avances Técnicos Cenicafé 388 · <a href="https://biblioteca.cenicafe.org/bitstream/10778/381/1/avt0388.pdf">PDF</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Zhang et al., 2019 – Processing parameters in Yunnan.</strong> Compared depulped and fermented coffee with coffee that was mechanically demucilaged before tank holding. Demucilaged coffee had far less substrate in the tank water but a comparable cup.</p>
<p class="meta">Frontiers in Microbiology 10, 2621 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2019.02621">doi:10.3389/fmicb.2019.02621</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Elhalis et al., 2021 – Wet fermentation and flavour.</strong> Compared 36-hour underwater fermentation with mechanical processing and no fermentation. Fermented beans carried more fermentation volatiles and were scored higher by a Q-grader panel.</p>
<p class="meta">European Food Research and Technology 247, 485–498 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s00217-020-03641-6">doi:10.1007/s00217-020-03641-6</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Oliveros-Tascón et al., 2011 – ECOLAV washer.</strong> Built an upflow washer for fermented coffee from the DESLIM design. Comparing the two shows how much residence time and energy the mechanical removal of intact mucilage needs.</p>
<p class="meta">Revista de Ingeniería 33, 61–67 · <a href="https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/1210/121022658008.pdf">PDF</a></p>
</div>
