## Why harvest timing matters

Coffee cherries ripen once or twice a year, and the months differ from origin to origin. The harvest in Brazil, the largest producer<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>, runs from April (robusta) or May (arabica) to about September.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In Colombia the main harvest is October to December<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>, in Vietnam late October to January<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>, and in Peru April to September, with about 85% of the crop picked from April to July.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Because the seasons are spread over the year, some origin is harvesting in every month (see the calendar below).

The harvest also sets when coffee can be shipped. In the Central African Republic, for example, cherries are picked from November to January; the first shipments leave in January or February and exports run from February to August.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>

## Main crop and second crop

Many origins harvest twice. In Colombia the main harvest runs from October to December and a second crop, the mitaca, comes from April to June, mainly in the central coffee region.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Kenya picks two crops, from April to June and from October to December.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> In Indonesia, arabica is harvested in April and May and again from September or October.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> In Papua New Guinea the main harvest runs from May to July, but enough cherries ripen from March to October for additional picking.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

In Uganda harvesting goes on all year, with two peaks: November to February and June to September.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> The main and minor harvests swap places between regions, depending on whether they lie north or south of the equator.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup>

## Altitude and region

A single country can harvest over many months. In Costa Rica the harvest is concentrated from November to February, but it starts around August in the lower south and can end around May in some high areas.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> In Guatemala it usually begins at the end of November and runs to March; it comes later in high areas such as Antigua and Huehuetenango and a little earlier in the lowlands.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> In Honduras the picking season runs from October to March, depending on the altitude of the farm.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup> In Rwanda, picking starts in late February on the shores of Lake Kivu and in early March elsewhere, and some northern areas pick until August.<sup><a href="#src-s15">15</a></sup>

Robusta and arabica in the same country can ripen at different times. In India arabica is harvested from November to January and robusta from December to February<sup><a href="#src-s16">16</a></sup>; in Laos arabica from October to December and robusta from December to February.<sup><a href="#src-s17">17</a></sup>

## How old the sources are

The calendar uses the most recent official statement we could find for each country. For most of the large producers this is a USDA Foreign Agricultural Service Coffee Annual from 2023–2025.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s8">8</a>,<a href="#src-s16">16</a></sup> For several African origins the most recent official country-wide calendar we found is the FAO country profile of 2000, built on 1999 data.<sup><a href="#src-s18">18</a></sup> The tool shows the year of every source with each country; these older rows should be read as the traditional season, not a current measurement.

China and Venezuela are not yet in the calendar: we found no official source that states their harvest months.
