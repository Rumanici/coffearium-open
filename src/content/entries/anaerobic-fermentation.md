## What anaerobic means in coffee

Anaerobic means “without oxygen”. In coffee processing, the word describes fermentation in a closed vessel that keeps outside air away from the coffee. Producers and researchers use three set-ups:

- **Self-induced anaerobic fermentation (SIAF)** – the coffee is closed in a tank or bucket. Microbes consume the oxygen that is inside, and carbon dioxide (CO₂) slowly builds up.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> An airlock lets the gas escape and stops outside air from coming in.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>
- **Air removed at the start** – the coffee is packed in plastic bags and the air is drawn out with a vacuum pump.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>
- **Air replaced with CO₂** – after the vacuum step, pure CO₂ is pumped into the bags.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Studies using this set-up often call it carbonic maceration, which has its own entry.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a>,<a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>

The opposite is an open tank. A 2025 Colombian study calls fermentation in open containers “semi-anaerobic”.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> In practice, “anaerobic” means low in oxygen, not free of it. The closed vessel limits oxygen and favours microbes that can live with or without it.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

## Whole cherries or pulped coffee

Anaerobic fermentation can start from whole cherries or from pulped coffee. Both can be fermented dry or with added water.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Two Brazilian trials, for example, compared whole and pulped coffee, each without water or with water making up 30% of the volume.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

The choice changes what the microbes can feed on. Whole cherries keep their sugary pulp. In one trial, the juice of whole cherries started at 23.65 °Brix, against 7.33 °Brix for pulped coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> °Brix is a measure of dissolved solids, mostly sugars.

Vessel sizes in the trials range from 20-litre buckets with lids to 200-litre tanks.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Runs lasted from one day to eight days.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> That is far longer than ordinary fermentation of washed coffee, for which the Codex code of practice gives 12 to 36 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup> The eight-day Colombian runs were designed to test fermentation beyond the 96 hours of earlier studies; the traditional control in the same trial took about 21 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

## What happens in the closed tank

The steps resemble those of ordinary fermentation, which has its own entry. Sugars are used up, acids and alcohols form, and the pH falls.

- **pH falls.** In a Brazilian tank of pulped coffee, the pH fell from 5.8 to 4.4.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In another trial, it fell from about 5.1 to as low as 3.70 within 72 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>
- **Lactic acid builds up.** Lactic acid was the main acid formed in several trials.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> In a Colombian comparison, the sealed tanks used more glucose and made more lactic acid than open tanks.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Alcohol builds up.** Green coffee fermented for 72 hours held about six times more ethanol than coffee fermented for 24 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **The mass stays cooler.** In a room held at 20 °C, the temperature differences (ΔT) reported were only 0.5–3.7 °C for sealed tanks, against 5.1–17.6 °C for open tanks.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

## Drying afterwards

After the tank, the coffee is dried like any other coffee. Whole cherries can be dried straight away, as in natural coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Other trials wash the coffee to stop fermentation before drying.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> In the trials, coffee was dried on raised beds or racks to about 11–12% moisture.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

Producers often name the result after both steps, for example “anaerobic natural” or “anaerobic washed”. One 2026 study called anaerobic fermentation the most common of the newer processing methods.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>

## Effect on the cup and the risks

Most anaerobic coffees in the trials scored as specialty coffee. In one trial, all SIAF coffees scored 84 points or more.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Some trials found gains over conventional coffee, but a 2025 Colombian trial did not.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Pooling 22 studies, a 2025 meta-analysis found that fermentation raised SCA scores by 0.6–1.4 points on average, most with oxygen-limited systems and longer runs.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> In that trial, sealed tanks gave more tropical fruit notes, and whole cherries in open tanks more citrus and berry notes.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

Long fermentation has costs. The seed is alive, and it can die in the tank.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Mycotoxin-producing fungi became more common as fermentation went on.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> One open-tank sample had a phenolic defect.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

## Oxygen, carbon dioxide and the vessel

**How the oxygen disappears.** In SIAF, nothing is pumped in or out. Microorganisms consume the available oxygen as part of their metabolism,<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> and CO₂ from microbial metabolism builds up gradually, limiting oxygen and favouring facultative microorganisms.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The vessel is closed with an airlock that lets CO₂ escape and keeps air out.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> None of the five SIAF trials consulted for this entry that describe their vessels reports a measured oxygen concentration;<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a>,<a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> the oxygen status is inferred from the set-up.

**Air removed or replaced.** In a 2026 trial in Yunnan, China, Catimor cherries were sealed in plastic bags and the air was removed by vacuum; the bags were kept closed for 7 days.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> In a 2022 trial with Conilon (*C. canephora*), the air was removed from 40 × 50 cm bags with a vacuum sealer, CO₂ of 99.9% purity was injected, and the bags were held at 18, 28 or 38 °C for 24–120 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The authors called this carbonic maceration.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> A 2023 review describes carbonic maceration as intact coffee sealed in an enclosed space with oxygen expelled and CO₂ added.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> How this differs from the wine technique of the same name is treated under carbonic maceration; adding starter cultures or other ingredients is treated under co-fermentation.

**Open versus sealed.** A 2025 Colombian trial compared open containers (semi-anaerobic, SA) and sealed hermetic tanks with an airlock (SIAF), each with whole cherries and with pulped coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> It used 30-litre high-density polyethylene bioreactors in a room held at 20 ± 1 °C and opened one bioreactor every 24 hours for up to 192 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

**Layers in a tall tank.** In a tank 82 cm high and 47 cm wide, holding about 175 kg of pulped coffee in 30 L of water, samples from taps at the bottom, middle and top differed significantly in their metabolites.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Lactic acid at 72 hours reached 5.06, 4.70 and 2.18 g/L in the bottom, middle and top layers.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The authors suggest stirring the tank to even out microbial metabolism.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Microbial pathways and metabolites

The groups of microbes involved are described under microorganisms. This section covers what the closed tank changes.

**Lactic fermentation.** Lactic acid was the most abundant acid in several trials that measured it.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> In the Colombian comparison, SIAF notably promoted glucose consumption and lactic acid production compared with open tanks.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> After 180 hours of SIAF without yeast inoculation and drying, natural coffee held 13.20 g/kg lactic acid; none was found at the start.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

**Alcoholic fermentation.** Ethanol accumulates in the seed with time: after 72 hours of SIAF, beans held about six times more ethanol than after 24 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Total alcohols were highest in whole cherries fermented without water, at 62.05 mg/g after 96 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

**Acetic acid.** In one SIAF trial, acetic acid appeared after 18 hours, although acetic acid bacteria stayed below 1% of the bacterial reads; the authors attribute it to lactic acid bacteria.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the Colombian comparison, acetic acid bacteria (mainly *Acetobacter*) made up 20–57% of the community in open tanks and were less abundant in sealed ones.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> In another trial, acetic acid peaked at 48 hours and then fell.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

**2,3-Butanediol.** In bacteria, 2,3-butanediol is made from pyruvate through acetolactate and acetoin; in the bacterium studied, it is one of several fermentative routes used under oxygen limitation to recycle reduced cofactors.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> In green coffee from a SIAF trial, 2,3-butanediol was detected at all three fermentation times.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Which organisms made it in the coffee tank was not tested.

**Undesirable acids.** Butyric acid is named among the undesirable products of prolonged fermentation.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> In two Brazilian SIAF trials, butyric and propionic acids were not detected in any treatment.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> A 2023 review lists sulphur and phenolic compounds and acetic acid among the defective volatiles that over-fermentation can produce.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>

**Esters and altitude.** A 2023 review states that fermentation favours the formation of esters such as ethyl acetate.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> In whole-cherry SIAF of one variety from one Brazilian region, coffee from lower altitudes had more volatile alcohols, and coffee from higher altitudes more esters, aldehydes and phenolics.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

## Monitoring

The trials measured the same quantities as ordinary fermentation; the end-point methods are described under fermentation. A closed vessel needs a sampling route: the Brazilian tank had three taps at different heights,<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> and the Colombian trial sacrificed one sealed bioreactor per sampling time.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

- **pH** – measured directly in the fermenting mass with a wireless meter with temperature compensation.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Starting and end values are in the reference protocol above.
- **Temperature** – logged with dataloggers.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Open containers warmed far more than sealed tanks; whole cherries in open containers stayed above 30 °C from 48 hours onward.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Sugars** – glucose by test strip,<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> or soluble solids (°Brix) followed until stable.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Time** – trials used fixed times rather than a texture end point.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

## Chemistry and sensory results

**Pooled evidence.** A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 22 studies published 2020–2025, with 236 comparisons against unfermented controls (or spontaneous fermentation, for starter cultures). Fermentation raised SCA scores by 0.6–1.4 points. In its meta-regression, semicarbonic systems scored higher than open tanks (by 1.84 points with starter cultures sprayed on, 1.54 with spontaneous fermentation), runs of 121 hours or more scored higher than runs under 36 hours (by 1.57 points), and fully anaerobic conditions scored lower than aerobic ones (by 1.22 points). The processing method (washed, natural or honey) had no independent effect. The model explained 21.3% of the variation between studies; the authors rated the risk of bias as moderate, with weak reporting of sensory panels, roast degree and temperature control.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup>

Results from controlled trials, by the variable each one tested:

- **Fermentation time.** In pulped coffee, beverages from 24, 48 and 72 hours of SIAF all scored 84 points or more; time changed aroma, flavour, acidity, body, overall and balance, but not sweetness, clean cup or uniformity.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Whole versus pulped, water versus none.** Scores ranged from 78.95 to 87.50; the highest, 87.50, came from whole cherries fermented without water for 96 hours, against 83.30 for the natural control and 83.60 for the pulped control.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Yeast starters in SIAF.** Over 180 hours, the best scores were 86.50 (pulped, *Torulaspora delbrueckii*) and 85.90 (natural, *Candida parapsilosis*); conventional coffees scored lowest, at 83.75 (natural) and 84.25 (pulped).<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Open versus sealed.** Apart from one phenolic sample, scores ranged from 80.75 to 85.00 SCA points, and the treatments did not significantly affect sensory quality.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Sealed tanks gave more tropical notes (yellow fruits, mango, pineapple, peach); whole cherries in open containers more citrus and berry notes.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Four methods on one lot.** In Peru, Catimor coffee scored 87.38 anaerobic, 85.75 natural, 84.00 washed and 82.88 honey, against 82.50 for a control sample (the abstract does not describe the anaerobic method or the control).<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> In Yunnan, anaerobic-then-washed and anaerobic-then-natural coffees scored higher than conventional coffees for aroma, flavour, aftertaste, acidity, body and balance; washed coffee was cleaner.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>
- **CO₂-filled bags (Conilon).** Scores ranged from 78.51 to 83.25 and were highest at 38 °C for 120 hours; scores rose significantly with temperature at 24, 72 and 96 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

## Risks and defects

- **Seed death.** In the Colombian trial, embryo viability stayed above 80% for up to 120 hours in sealed tanks but fell to as low as 25% by 192 hours in open tanks.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> After 180 hours of SIAF and drying, viability measured by the tetrazolium test fell from 85–87.5% in fresh coffee to 52.5–70%, while conventionally processed natural coffee kept 90%.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The role of the living seed is covered under seed physiology.
- **Physical quality.** The share of healthy beans was markedly lower under open-tank conditions, and the authors advise against open-tank fermentation of whole cherries beyond 24 hours.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Phenolic cup.** Of all samples in the Colombian trial, only one – whole cherries in an open tank for 72 hours – had a phenolic defect.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Fungi and ochratoxin A.** Longer fermentation brought a greater presence of mycotoxin-producing fungal genera. *Aspergillus* was most abundant in open tanks at 192 hours, and *Fusarium* predominated in sealed tanks of pulped coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Ochratoxin A stayed below 0.8 µg/kg, and SIAF showed no ochratoxin A production.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>
- **Uneven tanks.** Metabolites differed between the top, middle and bottom of a tall tank.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Off-flavours of over-fermentation.** Butyric acid, sulphur and phenolic compounds and excess acetic acid are named as undesirable products of prolonged or excessive fermentation.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> The classic fermentation defects are described under fermentation.

## Open questions

- **Real oxygen levels.** The SIAF trials consulted for this entry describe the vessel but report no oxygen measurement, so how anaerobic the tanks become, and how fast, is not established.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a>,<a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>
- **Score gains.** Pooled across 22 studies, fermentation raised scores modestly, oxygen-limited systems more than open tanks, but fully anaerobic conditions less than aerobic ones; why sealed systems underperform on average, and where self-induced anaerobic fermentation falls between these categories, is not settled.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> Single trials also disagree (see Contested).<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Origin of 2,3-butanediol.** It was detected in SIAF green coffee,<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> but which organisms made it was not tested.
- **Fungi in long runs.** *Fusarium* predominated in long SIAF runs even though ochratoxin A stayed low; what this means for safety and flavour is not settled.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Soares et al., 2025 – Meta-analysis of 22 studies.</strong> Pools 236 comparisons of fermented coffee with controls, published 2020–2025. Fermentation raised SCA scores by 0.6–1.4 points; oxygen-limited semicarbonic systems and runs of 121 hours or more gained most, fully anaerobic conditions less than aerobic ones. Most of the variation between studies stayed unexplained.</p>
<p class="meta">Trends in Food Science &amp; Technology 163, 105151 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tifs.2025.105151">doi:10.1016/j.tifs.2025.105151</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>da Silva Vale et al., 2023 – SIAF in a tall tank.</strong> Follows pulped Catuaí coffee in Brazil through 24, 48 and 72 hours of SIAF, sampling three heights of one tank. Enterobacteria and filamentous fungi were prominent, lactic acid differed between layers, ethanol in the beans rose about sixfold from 24 to 72 hours, and every cup scored 84 points or more.</p>
<p class="meta">Foods 12(1), 37 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/foods12010037">doi:10.3390/foods12010037</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Peñuela-Martínez et al., 2025 – Oxygen availability and time.</strong> Compares open containers and sealed SIAF tanks, with whole cherries and pulped coffee, for up to 192 hours in Colombia. Sealed tanks stayed cooler, made more lactic acid and kept the embryo alive longer; cup scores did not differ significantly between treatments.</p>
<p class="meta">Foods 14(17), 3001 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/foods14173001">doi:10.3390/foods14173001</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Buitrago-Zuluaga et al., 2025 – Fungi in prolonged fermentation.</strong> Identifies filamentous fungi in the same long open and sealed fermentations. Mycotoxin-producing genera became more common with time, <em>Aspergillus</em> led in open tanks and <em>Fusarium</em> in sealed tanks of pulped coffee, while ochratoxin A stayed below 0.8 µg/kg.</p>
<p class="meta">Applied Microbiology 5(4), 114 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/applmicrobiol5040114">doi:10.3390/applmicrobiol5040114</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Jimenez et al., 2023 – SIAF, yeasts and seed viability.</strong> Runs 180-hour SIAF of natural and pulped coffee with and without yeast starters in Brazil. Inoculated SIAF coffees scored up to 86.50 against 83.75–84.25 for conventional coffee, but every SIAF treatment lowered seed viability.</p>
<p class="meta">Food Bioscience 51, 102218 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fbio.2022.102218">doi:10.1016/j.fbio.2022.102218</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Silva et al., 2024 – Whole or pulped, dry or submerged.</strong> Ferments Catiguá MG2 coffee in sealed 200-litre bioreactors for up to 96 hours. Whole cherries fermented without water gave the highest score, 87.50, and butyric and propionic acids were not detected.</p>
