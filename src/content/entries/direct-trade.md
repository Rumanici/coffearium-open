## What direct trade means

Direct trade is a way of buying green coffee used by specialty roasters. The roaster buys from a known producer, cooperative or exporter in the producing country.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> A coffee trade textbook chapter says that some of these buyers even buy directly from individual farmers.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

According to that chapter, these buyers want to know who produced the coffee and how much that person was paid.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Third-wave roasters often call this kind of purchasing direct trade.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The wave labels are explained in Eras and waves of coffee.

“Relationship coffee” is a close term. A 2018 study defines it as long-term partnerships between coffee buyers and smallholders, based on product quality.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Another 2018 study links it to the direct engagement of roasting firms with producer communities.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Both stress a long-term relationship, not a single purchase.

## Who defines it

No law and no certification scheme defines direct trade. A 2020 systematic review states that no third-party certifier regulates the practice.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It therefore has no single definition and no fixed set of rules.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> No regulator's definition was found for this entry either (as of October 2026).

The review found that a few companies sought third-party audits or trademarks for the term, with mixed success.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It also reports that some pioneering companies have reduced or dropped their use of the term.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

The review sorts the practice into two types.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

- **Relational direct trade:** a direct and long-term relationship between roaster and producer, built on mutual trust.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Transactional direct trade:** a “hands off” form with little engagement, in which the producer alone bears losses from unforeseen events.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

Unlike a certification scheme, direct trade carries no standard or third-party audit of its own.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The schemes that do are compared in Certifications and labels.

## How the coffee moves

The sources consulted suggest that direct trade shortens the path of information more than the path of the coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> The 2020 review states that other actors are still needed in producing and importing countries.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> A 2017 discussion paper on the third-wave market makes the same point: even direct trade involves exporters and importers.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

The textbook describes how such roasters buy. They buy free on board (FOB) at the port of export and organise freight and insurance themselves.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> They import their own containers and normally have logistics, buying and quality teams.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Conventional roasters rely almost exclusively on importers and want delivery to the port nearest their plant.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> What FOB means for cost and risk is explained in Incoterms for coffee.

Smallholders face productivity and transaction constraints that keep them out of higher-value markets.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> A 2015 study argues that intermediaries are needed to connect them with specialty roasters.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> It studied three such “connective businesses”.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

Export itself follows the rules of the producing country. Ethiopia, for example, widened the right to export directly in a reform described in a 2018 report of the US Department of Agriculture.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Details are in the Deep dive.

## What studies found

Few studies have measured what direct trade changes for producers. The 2020 review of 95 publications reports no measured price or income outcomes and states that direct trade still lacks studies on its real benefits.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Three studies give field evidence.

**Colombia.** A 2018 study compared smallholders in a relationship-coffee programme with non-participants.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> Participants used more sustainable resource management practices and had better access to credit.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> They were also better informed and more optimistic about the coffee business.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> The study found no significant difference in farm-gate prices.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

**Indonesia.** A 2018 study looked at three coffee-growing communities on Sulawesi, Bali and Java.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Relationship coffee gave producers opportunities to upgrade.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> But key individuals in the communities captured these benefits.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> The authors conclude that it reproduced local inequality rather than reducing poverty.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>

**Guatemala.** According to a 2017 discussion paper, most farmers interviewed wanted more direct connections with the market.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Its author describes the shift of power towards producers as tenuous.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

No study consulted for this entry measured household income under direct trade against a comparison group. How value is shared along the chain is covered in Who earns what along the chain.

## Direct-trade claims on bags and menus

“Direct trade” on a bag or menu is a claim by the seller about its own buying. No certification stands behind the term itself.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The 2020 review lists misuse of the term to sell products among its limitations.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

In the EU, a “sustainability label” is “any voluntary trust mark, quality mark or equivalent, either public or private, that aims to set apart and promote a product, a process or a business by reference to its environmental or social characteristics, or both” (Article 2(q) of the amended Unfair Commercial Practices Directive).<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> Since 27 September 2026, “displaying a sustainability label that is not based on a certification scheme or not established by public authorities” is an unfair commercial practice in all circumstances (Annex I, point 2a).<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> A roaster's own direct-trade seal would probably count as such a label. That is our reading of the text; no court decision on it was found.

Written statements about how coffee was bought fall under the general ban on misleading commercial practices.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> The rules, and the Swiss and other national rules, are set out in Sustainability claims and greenwashing rules. This entry is not legal advice.

## Who carries the risk

In the sources consulted, direct trade does not remove price, quality or delivery risk; it moves it between buyer and seller, and where it lands depends on the contract.

**Fixed prices before harvest.** According to a coffee textbook chapter, forward contracts agreed before harvest tell producers and exporters what price they will receive and can serve as collateral for working capital.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> If a cooperative signs at a set price and market prices then rise, its members are tempted to sell to passing middlemen with ready money.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> The cooperative may then default on its contract; the chapter calls such defaults rare.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

**Quality and yield.** A 2024 operations-research model studies a roaster that contracts a grower's area before harvest.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> In the model, the roaster buys only coffee that meets the contracted quality specification.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> It is a model whose numerical examples use assumed parameters, not a field study.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup>

**Low engagement.** In the review's transactional type, the producer alone bears losses from unforeseen events.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> High search costs, language and cultural barriers, quality and supply risks and weak or informal contracts are among the limitations the literature names.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

**Capital.** The textbook describes investor-owned coffee companies whose suppliers finance their inventory for long periods.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> According to it, this model quickly eliminates direct buying from producers, small exporters and independent traders with limited capital.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

Contract terms themselves are covered in Green coffee contracts, and fixed versus exchange-linked prices in Differentials.

## Export rules at origin

Who may sell abroad is set by the producing country. In Ethiopia, a 2018 report of the US Department of Agriculture describes a reform approved by parliament in the July before the report.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> It allowed washing stations, mills and producers with more than 2 ha of coffee, producing at least 1 t per hectare, to export directly.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> They no longer had to sell through the Ethiopia Commodity Exchange (ECX).<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Commercial farmers and cooperative unions could already export directly before.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

The report states that the change broadened the number of companies that can export higher-value, traceable coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> No study consulted for this entry measured its effect on prices paid to producers. Rules in other producing countries were not consulted for this entry.

## Price transparency data

No study consulted for this entry checked prices that buyers publish themselves against contracts, so such prices are not used here.

The Specialty Coffee Transaction Guide, run by a team at Emory University, collects contract data from data donors on a confidential basis.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> It reports FOB prices because most export contracts report them consistently.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> Its first data brief notes that FOB prices tell only part of the economic story, and that its data donors are not necessarily representative of all specialty buyers.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> The brief lists the length of buying relationships as a question the data could address, but does not answer it.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> How the guide's prices relate to the exchange is explained in How a coffee price is made.

## What the literature discusses

The 2020 review coded 95 publications: 56 academic (theses and articles) and 39 technical (opinion articles, books and chapters).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It searched five academic databases plus the SCA website and one trade magazine.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The chart shows how often each theme was mentioned. Under accessibility, the review notes that high initial investments limit who can take part: medium-sized producers have been the main beneficiaries, while small ones face barriers.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It also notes that downstream actors keep power over quality and pricing.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The 2017 discussion paper calls the disintermediation of direct trade symbolic as much as logistic.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

## Open questions

- **Income effects.** No study consulted for this entry compared household income under direct trade with a matched comparison group.
- **Indirect premiums.** The Colombian study suggests premiums may reach producers through their cooperative rather than the farm-gate price; no study consulted measured this.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>
- **Scale.** No source consulted for this entry measured how much of the world's coffee is bought under direct trade, with any shared definition.
- **Relationship length and price.** The Specialty Coffee Transaction Guide names this as a question its data could answer; no published answer was consulted.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>

## Key studies

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Hernandez-Aguilera et al., 2018 – The Relationship Coffee Model in Colombia.</strong> Compared smallholders selling under a long-term, quality-based buying relationship with non-participants, using matching to account for differences between them. Participants used more sustainable practices, had better access to credit and were better informed, but farm-gate prices did not differ significantly.</p>
<p class="meta">Business Strategy and the Environment 27(2), 179–198 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1002/bse.2009">doi:10.1002/bse.2009</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Vicol et al., 2018 – Upgrading for whom?</strong> Three case studies of coffee communities on Sulawesi, Bali and Java, Indonesia, where roasters engage directly with producers. Relationship coffee created upgrading opportunities, but key individuals captured the benefits, and local inequality was reproduced.</p>
<p class="meta">World Development 110, 26–37 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.worlddev.2018.05.020">doi:10.1016/j.worlddev.2018.05.020</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Guimarães et al., 2020 – Direct trade: contributions and limitations.</strong> A systematic review of 95 academic and technical publications on direct trade in specialty coffee. It finds no single definition and no regulator, proposes relational and transactional types, and groups the limitations into implementation, definition and access.</p>
<p class="meta">Internext 15(3), 34–62 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.18568/internext.v15i3.588">doi:10.18568/internext.v15i3.588</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Borrella et al., 2015 – Connective businesses.</strong> A cross-case study of three businesses that link smallholders with specialty roasters. It argues that productivity and transaction constraints keep smallholders out of higher-value markets without such intermediaries.</p>
<p class="meta">IDS Bulletin 46(3), 29–44 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1111/1759-5436.12142">doi:10.1111/1759-5436.12142</a></p>
</div>
