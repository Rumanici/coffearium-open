## How coffee consumption is counted

No one counts the cups. Coffee consumption by country is an estimate built from trade, production and stock data. The International Coffee Organization (ICO) calls it apparent consumption: production plus net trade and changes in stocks.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> As apparent consumption, the figures fluctuate from year to year.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

For importing countries, the ICO uses a measure it calls disappearance. Re-exports are deducted from imports, and the result is adjusted for changes in visible inventories.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Inventories here are stocks of green coffee held by importers and roasters.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> For producing countries, the ICO uses domestic consumption estimates provided by its members.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

Coffee crosses borders as green, roasted and soluble (instant) coffee. To add them up, statistics convert everything to green bean equivalent (GBE). The US Department of Agriculture (USDA) multiplies roasted coffee by 1.19 and soluble coffee by 2.6.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Volumes are given in bags of 60 kg of green coffee.

The two main datasets use different years. The ICO reports by coffee year, from October to September.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> USDA uses marketing years, which for many producing countries follow their own crop year.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Crop year and coffee year explains the difference.

## The largest consumers

The ICO estimates world consumption at 182.2 million bags in coffee year 2024/25, up 4.3% on the year before.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> For 2025/26 it expects a decline of 0.8%, partly a normalisation after unusually strong growth.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The ICO names the United States as the world’s largest coffee-consuming country.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

USDA counts 173.5 million bags in 2025/26, about 10.4 million t of green coffee (our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> USDA lists the European Union as one bloc. Its 27 countries drank 41.6 million bags, the United States 25.6 million and Brazil 22.3 million.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> These three drink 51.5% of the world total (our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

After the three leaders, the field is close. Japan, the Philippines and China each drank 6.4–6.7 million bags in 2025/26.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> For 2026/27, USDA forecasts a record 179.7 million bags, with the largest gains in the European Union and the United States.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

By region, Europe drank the most: 30.1% of world consumption in 2024/25, according to the ICO.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Asia and Oceania followed with 48.9 million bags, North America with 30.2 million and South America with 28.8 million.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> For 2025/26 the ICO expects South America to overtake North America as the third-largest region.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

## Coffee per person

Totals favour large countries. Dividing consumption by population shows how much coffee an average person drinks. The ICO calculates per capita consumption in this way, from disappearance or domestic consumption.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

For the chart below, we divided USDA consumption for 2025/26 by World Bank population for 2025 (our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The world average is about 1.3 kg of green coffee per person per year. Switzerland reaches 7.9 kg, Brazil 6.3 kg and the United States 4.5 kg. China stands at 0.27 kg and India at 0.06 kg.

These are kilograms of green coffee, not of roasted coffee or cups. A kilogram of roasted coffee counts as 1.19 kg of green coffee in USDA tables.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Per-person figures from other bodies may use roasted weight, so they are not directly comparable.

Trading hubs need care. Belgium imported 282,000 t of green coffee in 2024, according to a Dutch government market study citing Eurostat.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Divided by its population, that would be 23.8 kg per person (our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> Yet most of it is re-exported, mainly to other European countries.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Imports alone are therefore not consumption.

## Producing countries and Asia

Producing countries drink a growing part of their crop. Brazil is the second-largest consuming country after the United States in the USDA table.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> How much each producer keeps at home is set out in Coffee production by country.

Several producing and Asian countries have grown fast. Between 2021/22 and 2025/26, Vietnam’s consumption rose from 3.2 to 4.9 million bags (+53%, our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Ethiopia rose from 3.4 to 4.5 million bags and China from 4.9 to 6.4 million bags.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> USDA lowered its Ethiopian export estimate because more coffee than expected was drunk at home.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

</figure>

Not every Asian market grew in this period. South Korea, which USDA does not list as a producer, stayed between 3.2 and 3.4 million bags.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Indonesia, a large producer, stayed at about 4.8 million bags.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> USDA revises its estimates between reports, and the July 2026 circular lists such revisions.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

Ways of drinking coffee differ widely between these countries. Brewing traditions are described in Coffee drinks around the world and The Ethiopian coffee ceremony.

## Disappearance and apparent consumption

The ICO’s Statistics Committee defines disappearance as the level of consumption in importing members. It is measured by deducting re-exports from imports and adjusting for changes in visible inventories.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Net imports equal gross imports less re-exports.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> In non-member countries, the ICO treats net imports as actual consumption.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Per capita consumption is disappearance, or domestic consumption in exporting countries, divided by population, which the ICO takes from the International Monetary Fund.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

For producing countries, members provide the estimates. If no recent estimate exists for the current crop year, the ICO uses the previous year’s.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> In its export statistics, for exporting members that produce or export both species, the ICO converts roasted and soluble coffee into GBE and splits it 50/50 into arabica and robusta.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Its Coffee Market Report summarises the result as apparent consumption: production plus net trade and changes in stocks, where applicable.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

USDA tables give production, imports, exports, consumption and ending stocks by country. For each non-producing country, it uses the balance of imports and exports to avoid double counting.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Switzerland shows why. In 2025/26, USDA counts Swiss imports of 3.5 million bags but consumption of 1.2 million.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Swiss exports of roasted and ground coffee came to 1.9 million bags in GBE.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> South Korea’s imports and consumption are both 3.2 million bags.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

| Step | ICO (importing members) | USDA |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Gross imports from all origins, in GBE<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> | Production, imports and exports by form in GBE<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> |
| Re-exports | Deducted (net imports)<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> | Trade balance used for non-producing countries<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> |
| Stocks | Adjusted for changes in visible inventories<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> | Ending stocks in the balance<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> |
| Year | Coffee year, October–September<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> | Marketing year; October–September for non-producers<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> |
| Conversion | Roasted ×1.19; soluble ×2.6 (2011 document)<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> | Roasted ×1.19; soluble ×2.6<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> |

## Re-export hubs and per-person figures

Hubs import far more coffee than they drink. Belgium is the largest European re-exporter of green coffee, according to the Dutch market study.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> It gives 282,000 t of green coffee imports and 184,000 t of green coffee re-exports for 2024, citing Eurostat.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> For the Belgian consumer market it gives a volume of 44,600 t in 2023, from a European Coffee Federation report, without saying whether this is roasted or green coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Divided by imports, Belgium would appear to drink 23.8 kg per person; divided by that market volume, 3.8 kg (both our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

Switzerland is the other well-known case. It imports mostly green coffee and exports mostly roasted coffee; Switzerland and the coffee trade explains the flows. The Dutch government study gives about 7.9 kg per person for 2020, without stating whether green or roasted.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Our calculation from USDA and World Bank data gives the same 7.9 kg for 2025/26.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> In the ICO’s 2004 series of per capita disappearance, Switzerland stood at 6.95 kg in 2003.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

Deducting re-exports removes the largest distortion, but not all of it. The 2004 ICO study notes that part of imports is destined for re-export.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> It adds that more precise analysis would need more detail on the structure of imports and re-exports.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> No source consulted for this entry measures how far the remaining gaps change the ranking of small hub countries. Germany, a large importer and re-exporter, is not shown separately in the USDA table, which reports the European Union as a whole.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Green, roasted and soluble coffee

How coffee is drunk shapes how it is traded. In 2025/26, USDA counts world exports of 145.9 million bags in GBE.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Of these, 119.5 million were green coffee, 19.9 million soluble coffee and 6.5 million roasted and ground (our sum and shares: 81.9%, 13.7% and 4.4%).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In 2025/26 the largest soluble exporters in the table are Brazil, Vietnam, the European Union, Malaysia and India.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

Some markets rely on soluble coffee. The Philippines imported 6.1 million bags in GBE in 2025/26, of which 5.5 million were soluble coffee (90.5%, our calculation).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> No source consulted for this entry gives the share of soluble coffee in consumption worldwide.

An older ICO study estimated the split between roasted and soluble coffee in eight importing countries. It applied Euromonitor market shares to ICO consumption totals, averaged over 1997–2010.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> On that basis, soluble coffee made up 8.8% of consumption in the United States, 35.8% in Japan, 79.8% in the United Kingdom and 88.8% in Russia.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> These shares are dated and rest on market-research data.

## Open questions

- **Comparable datasets.** ICO and USDA world totals for 2024/25 differ by about 10 million bags; no source consulted here reconciles them country by country (see Contested).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>
- **Hub countries.** No source consulted for this entry measures how much of the per-person figure for Switzerland, Belgium or Luxembourg reflects coffee bought by non-residents or stocks moving across borders.
- **Producing-country estimates.** Consumption in producing countries rests on member estimates and is revised between reports; no survey-based check of these estimates was found.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Form of coffee drunk.** The most recent roasted and soluble shares consulted here are averages for 1997–2010 and rest on market-research data.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
