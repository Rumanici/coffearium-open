## Why it matters for processing

The main processing methods are defined by what happens to these layers:

- In the **wet (washed) process**, the skin and pulp are removed mechanically in the presence of water.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> The mucilage left on the seeds is then removed, by fermentation or other methods, and washed off before drying.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> See [Washed process](/entries/washed-process/).
- In the **dry (natural) process**, the whole cherry is dried, in the sun or in drying machines, giving "husk coffee".<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> The dried husk – skin, pulp, mucilage and parchment – is removed afterwards, together with as much silverskin as possible.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> See [Natural process](/entries/natural-process/).

## By-products

Cascara (the dried husk), mucilage, parchment, silverskin and, after brewing, spent coffee grounds are the main by-products of coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Size and proportions

The coffee cherry is a drupe, about 1.5 cm across on average.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> Skin and pulp together make up about 30% of the cherry's dry matter.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> Dry processing gives roughly 1 kg of husk for every kilogram of coffee beans.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

## Which layers leave when

| Layer | Botanical name | Washed process | Natural process |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skin | Exocarp | Removed by the pulper, before drying | Dried on the seed, removed at hulling |
| Pulp | Mesocarp | Removed by the pulper, before drying | Dried on the seed, removed at hulling |
| Mucilage | – | Removed by fermentation or other methods, then washed off | Dried on the seed, removed at hulling |
| Parchment | Endocarp | Dried on the seed, removed at hulling | Removed at hulling, with the husk |
| Silverskin | – | Stays on the seed; detaches in roasting | As much as possible removed at hulling; the rest detaches in roasting |

Sources for the table: Codex code of practice<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>, Bobková et al.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>, Eckhardt et al.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

## Mucilage: what it is made of

Mucilage is mostly water. Figures compiled in a 2018 study give 85–91% water and 6.2–7.4% sugars, with small amounts of protein, non-reducing sugar and pectin.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> An earlier compilation cited in the same study gives 84.2% water, 8.9% protein, 4.1% sugar, 0.91% pectin and 0.7% ash.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Cenicafé, the Colombian coffee research centre, gives 15% solids, in the form of a water-insoluble colloidal gel without cellular structure; 80% of these solids are pectic acids and 20% sugars.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> A 2022 Cuban review gives a slightly higher water content and similar sugar and pectin values: "El principal componente del mucílago de café es la humedad (91,77%), seguido de los azúcares totales (6,43%), las pectinas (0,81%) y el nitrógeno (0,12%)" – the main component of coffee mucilage is moisture (91.77%), followed by total sugars (6.43%), pectins (0.81%) and nitrogen (0.12%).<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The sources disagree most on pectin (see Contested). See [Mucilage](/entries/mucilage/) for its structure and the other published figures.

## The seed is alive

The coffee seed is a living organism during processing. A 2007 study on Arabica tracked germination markers in seeds during processing and found that germination processes start, peak and fade during post-harvest treatment – with different timing and intensity for wet and dry processing.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> This is one reason why processing changes the chemistry of the seed itself, not only its surface. See [Washed process](/entries/washed-process/) and [Natural process](/entries/natural-process/) for the details.
