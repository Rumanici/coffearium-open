## A trading centre without coffee farms

Switzerland has no coffee plantations.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> In the International Coffee Organization (ICO) it is an importing member.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Yet a large part of the world’s green coffee is bought and sold by companies based there. In 2025 the Swiss government estimated that 55% of worldwide green coffee trade takes place via Swiss-based trading companies.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Other estimates range from at least half to 60–70%; the highest comes from a discussion paper that cites a news article.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> No source consulted for this entry explains how the share was measured (see Contested).

A 2013 report to the Federal Council names Geneva and Zug, and also Lugano, as centres of commodity trading.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> For coffee it cites trade-association figures: about half of the global coffee trade passes through the Lake Geneva region, and another 10% through Winterthur.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> According to a Dutch government market study (2022), the world’s largest green coffee importers all have trading offices in Switzerland.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

The tradition is old. Commodity merchanting in Switzerland goes back to the 18th century.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Some early trading houses, such as Volkart Brothers, had become holding companies by 2013.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Switzerland has been a member of the ICO since 1964.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Paper trade and physical trade

Most of this trade never touches Swiss soil. The Federal Council report defines merchanting: a company in Switzerland buys goods from a supplier abroad and sells them to a buyer abroad.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> As a rule, the goods do not cross the border into Switzerland, so no Swiss customs duty applies.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

A trading house in Geneva can therefore buy a lot from an exporter at origin and sell it to a roaster in another country. A lot is a batch of coffee from one origin and process, traded as a unit. The contracts, payments and price risk run through Switzerland; the ship sails from the origin port to the buyer’s port. How such contracts are written is explained in Green coffee contracts and Incoterms for coffee.

Coffee for Swiss roasters is a separate, physical flow. It mainly enters the country on the Rhine, through the port of Basel.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> In 2021, 99% of Swiss green coffee imports came directly from producing countries.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

## Green coffee in, roasted coffee out

Swiss customs statistics show a clear pattern. In 2019, 93% of the coffee imported was unroasted (basis not stated), while 98% of the coffee exported by volume was roasted.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> Switzerland imported 187,591 t of coffee worth CHF 745 million that year and exported 83,819 t worth CHF 2,492 million.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

Exports grew fast. In 2006 Switzerland exported 12,747 t of coffee worth CHF 202 million.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> By 2019 the export value had reached CHF 2.5 billion, a record at the time.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> In 2019 one kilo of exported coffee had an average customs value of CHF 29.70, one kilo imported CHF 4.00.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>

According to the Dutch government market study, the high value of Swiss roasted coffee exports is strongly related to single-serve Nespresso capsules.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> A 2024 university discussion paper attributes the growth of Swiss coffee exports most likely to Nespresso.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> No official statistic consulted for this entry separates capsules from other roasted coffee.

Part of the imports is drunk in Switzerland. Per capita coffee consumption was about 7.9 kg in 2020.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Domestic consumption in 2021 was about 64,000 t of green coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>

## Compulsory coffee stocks

Switzerland keeps emergency stocks of goods the Federal Council considers vital, to bridge serious shortages.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> Coffee is one of them, together with sugar and edible oils.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> Companies that import a minimum quantity must hold part of these stocks.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> By a decision of the Federal Council, coffee stocks must cover three months of demand.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>

The stocks are paid for by a levy on imports.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a>,<a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> As of October 2026, importers pay a guarantee fund contribution of CHF 0.12 per kg of green coffee and CHF 0.144 per kg of roasted coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a>,<a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> The cooperative réservesuisse manages the coffee guarantee fund.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a>,<a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> Coffee that is exported again is entitled to a refund from the guarantee fund.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> How the levy enters a landed cost is shown in How a coffee price is made.

In 2019 the authorities proposed to end compulsory coffee stocks. They argued that coffee was no longer a vital good.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a>,<a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> After the consultation, they saw no need for action, and the three-month requirement stayed.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> At the end of 2025, 17 companies stored 20,180 t of coffee, and stocks are being built up to 20,640 t by the end of 2026.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a>,<a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup>

## How the stockpiling system works

The legal basis is the National Economic Supply Act (Landesversorgungsgesetz, LVG), its ordinance and the ordinances on compulsory stocks of each sector.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> Target quantities, except for medicines, follow the average consumption of the past three years.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> There are five compulsory-stock organisations, and every company that must hold stocks is a member of one.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> Private industry organisations manage the guarantee funds that cover the cost of the stocks.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup> The Federal Office for National Economic Supply (Bundesamt für wirtschaftliche Landesversorgung, BWL) states that the costs are passed on to consumers through sales prices.<sup><a href="#src-s13">13</a></sup>

For coffee the fund is held by réservesuisse. Contributions are charged per kg by customs tariff number and were raised from 1 January 2024.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup> Contributions on coffee that leaves the country again are refunded; in 2025 about 174,000 t of exported coffee qualified.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> The levy therefore falls mainly on coffee consumed in Switzerland (our inference). At the end of 2025 the coffee fund held CHF 13.3 million, a reserve of 6.4 months against a rule of six months.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

## The 2019 debate on ending coffee stocks

In its 2019 consultation draft, the BWL proposed removing coffee from compulsory stockpiling because coffee was no longer to be classed as a vital good.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> At that time 15 companies held 15,300 t, and compulsory and free coffee stocks together covered an average of six months of normal consumption.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup> The six months referred to all coffee stocks; the compulsory requirement is set separately in months of demand.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a>,<a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

The consultation showed divided interests. Six of the 15 stockholders were in favour of keeping the stocks, but only with financial compensation; coffee importers without stocks opposed keeping them.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> An industry group argued that the stocks cost taxpayers nothing because the guarantee fund pays for them.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup>

The proposal did not go ahead. The BWL’s 2023 report records no need for action after the 2019 consultation and a Federal Council decision to keep the coverage at three months.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> In 2023 an increase from 18,750 t to 20,640 t by the end of 2026 was decided.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> As of the BWL’s report on 2025, the coffee stocks are being built up further in 2026 to reach the required coverage.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup>

## Estimates of the trade share and why they differ

The figures for Switzerland’s share of the world coffee trade come from different bodies and years. The 2013 Federal Council report takes its coffee figures from the Geneva Trading and Shipping Association.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The same report shows industry estimates of global market shares in a chart and notes that they could not be verified.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Public Eye (2019) and a 2024 university discussion paper give their own estimates, again without a stated method.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

The estimates also count different things. Some speak of trade passing through a region, some of trade dispatched from Switzerland, and the 2025 government letter of trade via Swiss-based companies.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> Merchanting does not appear in Swiss import statistics, because the goods do not cross the border.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Customs data therefore cannot confirm any of these shares. Coffearium gives the range and the government estimate, with dates.

## Sustainability and transparency debates

The Federal Council’s position, as set out in its report of 30 November 2018, is to keep Switzerland competitive and sound as a commodity location.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup> It aims to strengthen integrity and sustainability by promoting global standards.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup> The report states that the commodity sector still faces challenges in respecting human rights, and it stresses the need for quantitative data on the sector.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup> In 2017 merchanting receipts equalled 3.8% of Swiss gross domestic product (GDP), across all commodities.<sup><a href="#src-s14">14</a></sup>

Public Eye, a Swiss non-governmental organisation (NGO), sees the sector differently. It describes agricultural commodity trading as opaque and high-risk for human rights, and it calls the lack of data unacceptable.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> It argues that regulation of the sector is long overdue and that the government has ignored recommendations to regulate it.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>

For coffee, government and industry chose a voluntary, joint approach. The Swiss Sustainable Coffee Platform (SSCP) was launched in Bern on 6 June 2024.<sup><a href="#src-s15">15</a></sup> It brings together the Swiss coffee industry, the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), civil society and academia, with the aim of improving sustainability in the coffee sector.<sup><a href="#src-s15">15</a></sup> No source consulted for this entry has evaluated its results as of October 2026.

How the EU Deforestation Regulation affects Swiss companies that sell coffee into the European Union is covered in EUDR.

## Open questions

- **Measured trade share.** No official statistic measures the share of world green coffee trade contracted by Swiss-based companies; all figures consulted for this entry are estimates without a stated method.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s2">2</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Capsules in the trade statistics.** The official sources consulted here do not separate capsules from other roasted coffee, so the capsule share of Swiss export value is not established.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a>,<a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup>
- **Basis of the refund quantities.** The réservesuisse report does not say whether exported quantities entitled to a refund are counted as product weight or green-coffee equivalent.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>
- **Effect of the SSCP.** The platform started in June 2024; no evaluation of its projects was found.<sup><a href="#src-s15">15</a></sup>
