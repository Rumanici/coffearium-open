## What the evidence is made of

Almost everything known about coffee and long-term health comes from observational studies. These follow large groups of people, record how much coffee they drink and count who later develops a disease.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> They show associations: coffee drinkers may differ from non-drinkers in many other ways.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> Randomised trials, which could show cause, have given coffee only for short periods.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

A wide summary is a 2017 umbrella review, a review of meta-analyses. It covered 201 meta-analyses of observational research on 67 health outcomes and 17 meta-analyses of trials, in adults in any country.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> About a quarter of the evidence was rated low quality and three quarters very low quality, on the GRADE scale used to rate evidence.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> No authors of the review reported support from any organisation for the work.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Outcome by outcome

In the umbrella review, coffee drinking was more often associated with lower risk than with higher risk.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> For several outcomes the largest relative risk reductions were at about three to four cups a day, compared with none.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> A relative risk (RR) of 0.83 means a 17% lower risk in that group.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It is not proof that coffee caused the difference.

- **All-cause mortality:** RR 0.83 at three cups a day versus none.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Heart and blood vessels:** cardiovascular mortality RR 0.81 at three cups a day versus none; incident cardiovascular disease RR 0.85, with the largest benefit at three to five cups a day.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Type 2 diabetes:** RR 0.70 for high versus low intake.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Liver:** lower risk of chronic liver disease, cirrhosis and liver cancer.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Nervous system:** consistent associations with lower risk of Parkinson’s disease and of depression.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Bones:** in women, high versus low intake was associated with a higher risk of fracture (RR 1.14).<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

Pregnancy is the main exception. There, harmful associations remained after adjustment for smoking.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) intake statement for pregnancy, up to 200 mg of caffeine a day, is covered in *Caffeine*.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

## How much, and which coffee

A UK cohort shows what the shape of the association looks like. The UK Biobank followed 498,134 adults aged 38–73 for about 10 years.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> Compared with non-drinkers, every intake from one cup to eight or more cups a day was associated with lower all-cause mortality.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The hazard ratios differed little between these intake levels, from 0.84 to 0.92.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup>

In the same cohort, similar associations appeared for instant, ground and decaffeinated coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> They also held for people whose genes predict slower or faster caffeine breakdown.<sup><a href="#src-s2">2</a></sup> The umbrella review found no convincing harmful association for decaffeinated coffee with any outcome.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Which coffee compounds might matter is covered in *Bioactive compounds*.

Brewing method matters for one known effect. Unfiltered coffee, such as boiled coffee, raises blood cholesterol through the diterpenes cafestol and kahweol; paper filters hold them back.<sup><a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> See *Coffee oil* for the details.

## Why association is not cause

Four problems recur in coffee research.

- **Confounding.** People who drink more coffee have often smoked more. Many harmful associations largely disappeared with adequate adjustment for smoking.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> A 2020 narrative review of 36 cohort samples found that many did not adjust adequately for smoking.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Reverse causation.** Illness can change habits. In case-control studies, symptoms of disease may have led people to drink less coffee; other observational studies share this risk.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Measurement.** There is no standard cup size, so “cups” in different studies can mean different amounts.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Short trials.** Randomised trials have given coffee only for short durations and measured few outcomes, such as blood pressure, not long-term disease.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

How to judge a single new headline about coffee is the subject of *How to read coffee health news*.

## What health authorities say

Several bodies have assessed the evidence. Their statements, as published:

- **IARC (World Health Organization), 2016:** drinking coffee is “not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans (Group 3)”. In 1991 it had been rated “possibly carcinogenic” (Group 2B).<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a>,<a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>
- **World Cancer Research Fund and American Institute for Cancer Research (WCRF/AICR), 2018:** coffee “probably protects against liver cancer and endometrial cancer”.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup>
- **US Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee, 2015:** it graded as strong the evidence on moderate intake in healthy adults. By its conclusion, 3–5 cups a day (up to 400 mg caffeine) is not associated with higher risk of major chronic diseases or premature death.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>
- **EFSA, 2015:** habitual caffeine intake up to 400 mg a day raises no safety concerns for healthy non-pregnant adults; it assessed caffeine, not coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s10">10</a></sup>

Very hot drinks are a separate matter. The IARC classifies drinking beverages above 65 °C as probably carcinogenic (Group 2A); see *Serving temperature*.<sup><a href="#src-s6">6</a></sup> Roasting by-products such as acrylamide are covered in *Roasting contaminants*.

## The observational evidence in detail

The umbrella review re-analysed each meta-analysis and graded it.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> It found evidence of a non-linear association for several outcomes, with the largest relative risk reductions at three to four cups a day versus none.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Beyond the outcomes above, it reported lower risk of several specific cancers and of neurological, metabolic and liver conditions.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> For Parkinson’s disease and depression it describes consistent associations but gives no single summary estimate in its text.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

The 2015 US advisory committee reached similar conclusions from observational data. It graded as moderate the evidence that moderate coffee intake is associated with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, and that regular intake is associated with reduced risk of liver and endometrial cancer.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup> It also graded as moderate the evidence for an inverse association between caffeine intake and Parkinson’s disease.<sup><a href="#src-s8">8</a></sup>

For cancer, the WCRF/AICR dose–response meta-analysis found RR 0.86 for liver cancer per cup a day.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> The report names smoking and hepatitis B and C viruses as possible confounders or effect modifiers.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> It graded the evidence for a lower risk of cancers of the mouth, pharynx and larynx, and of some skin cancers, as limited–suggestive.<sup><a href="#src-s9">9</a></sup> IARC found evidence suggesting lack of carcinogenicity for cancers of the female breast, uterine endometrium, prostate, pancreas and liver.<sup><a href="#src-s7">7</a></sup>

## What genetic studies add

Mendelian randomisation (MR) uses gene variants linked to coffee intake as a stand-in for intake itself.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The umbrella review’s authors suggested it as a way to test whether residual confounding explains the observational findings.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> They also note one limit: the approach relies on an assumption of linearity.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

- **Cardiovascular disease.** A 2021 MR study used 12 variants and data from the UK Biobank (up to 35,979 cases) and the FinnGen consortium in Finland (up to 17,325 cases).<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> In the main (univariable) analysis, genetically predicted intake was not associated with any of 15 cardiovascular outcomes. The authors concluded that earlier observational studies may have been confounded.<sup><a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup>
- **Many outcomes.** A 2025 systematic review covered 59 MR studies and 160 disease or biomarker associations.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> It found probable evidence for a lower risk of hepatocellular carcinoma (the main liver cancer), ovarian cancer, migraine, kidney disease and gallstone disease.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Opposite directions.** The same review found probable evidence for a higher risk of type 2 diabetes, oesophageal and digestive cancers and Alzheimer’s disease, among other conditions, and for higher total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> It reports these as associations.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

The liver findings point the same way in observational and genetic studies.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> For cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes they do not.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup> The higher cholesterol in MR is in line with the trial evidence for unfiltered coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a>,<a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup> The 2025 review calls for more MR studies in non-European cohorts.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

## Randomised trials

Trials randomly assign who drinks coffee, so they reduce the risk of confounding.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> In the umbrella review, 17 meta-analyses of trials covered nine outcomes: blood pressure, blood lipids and three pregnancy outcomes.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Coffee was given for short durations only.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> The review found coffee consistently associated with unfavourable changes in the lipid profile, and brewing method is an important factor there.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup>

One 2023 randomised case-crossover trial, funded by a university and the US National Institutes of Health, shows what short trials can measure.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> One hundred adults wore heart, movement and glucose monitors for 14 days, and daily text messages told them at random to drink caffeinated coffee or avoid caffeine.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> Premature atrial contractions did not rise significantly (58 versus 53 a day). Premature ventricular contractions rose from 102 to 154 a day, daily steps rose from 9,665 to 10,646, and nightly sleep fell from 432 to 397 minutes.<sup><a href="#src-s12">12</a></sup> These are short-term effects in one study, not long-term outcomes.

## Biases in detail

- **Smoking.** In the umbrella review, harmful associations were largely nullified by adequate adjustment for smoking, and residual confounding by smoking, despite some adjustment, is named as a likely explanation for an apparent harm.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> A 2020 narrative review of cohort studies reported that 1–5 cups a day was related to lower mortality among never smokers and in studies that adjusted for pack-years of smoking.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup> It recommends separate analyses for never smokers.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Diet and sugar.** The same review notes that possible effects of sugar added to coffee have not been adequately investigated.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Reverse causality.** Bias from reverse causality can occur in observational studies, not only in case-control designs.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Exposure.** Cup sizes differ, so studies comparing cups may compare ranges of exposure.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup> Brewing method changes what is in the cup.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s11">11</a></sup>
- **Small-study bias.** Egger’s test for small-study effects could be run on only 40% of the meta-analyses re-analysed in the umbrella review.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Decaf drinkers.** People who drink decaffeinated coffee might differ from those who drink caffeinated coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>

## Open questions

- **Cause or confounding for the heart.** Observational data show lower cardiovascular risk; MR data show none.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s3">3</a></sup> No long-term trial consulted for this entry has tested cardiovascular events.
- **Type 2 diabetes.** Observational and MR results point in opposite directions.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a>,<a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>
- **Fracture in women.** The umbrella review suggests that women at increased risk of fracture should possibly be excluded from any trial of coffee.<sup><a href="#src-s1">1</a></sup>
- **Added sugar.** Possible effects of sugar added to coffee have not been adequately investigated.<sup><a href="#src-s5">5</a></sup>
- **Populations.** The 2025 MR review calls for more studies in non-European cohorts.<sup><a href="#src-s4">4</a></sup>

## Key studies

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Poole et al., 2017 – Umbrella review of coffee and health.</strong> Re-analysed 201 meta-analyses of observational studies and 17 of trials covering 67 outcomes. Coffee was more often associated with benefit than harm, with the largest risk reductions at three to four cups a day; most evidence was of low or very low quality, and pregnancy and fracture in women were exceptions.</p>
<p class="meta">BMJ 359, j5024 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.j5024">doi:10.1136/bmj.j5024</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Loftfield et al., 2018 – Coffee and mortality in the UK Biobank.</strong> Followed 498,134 UK adults for about 10 years. Every intake level from one to eight or more cups a day was associated with lower all-cause mortality than no coffee, for instant, ground and decaffeinated coffee alike and regardless of genetic caffeine-metabolism score.</p>
<p class="meta">JAMA Internal Medicine, 2018 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2018.2425">doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2018.2425</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Yuan et al., 2021 – Mendelian randomisation and cardiovascular disease.</strong> Used 12 genetic variants for coffee intake in UK and Finnish data. Genetically predicted intake was not associated with any of 15 cardiovascular outcomes, which the authors read as a sign that earlier observational findings may have been confounded.</p>
<p class="meta">Nutrients 13(7), 2218 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13072218">doi:10.3390/nu13072218</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Pham et al., 2025 – Systematic review of Mendelian randomisation studies.</strong> Reviewed 59 studies covering 160 disease or biomarker associations. It found probable evidence linking genetically predicted intake with lower risk of liver cancer and kidney disease but higher risk of type 2 diabetes and higher LDL cholesterol, partly against the observational picture.</p>
<p class="meta">Nutrition Research Reviews 38(2), 1006–1026 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954422425100206">doi:10.1017/s0954422425100206</a></p>
</div>

<div class="study">
<p><strong>Marcus et al., 2023 – Randomised coffee days.</strong> In this case-crossover trial, one hundred adults were told at random each day for 14 days to drink caffeinated coffee or avoid caffeine, while monitors recorded heart rhythm, movement and sleep. Coffee days brought more ventricular extra beats and steps and less sleep, but no significant rise in atrial extra beats.</p>
<p class="meta">New England Journal of Medicine, 2023 · <a href="https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2204737">doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2204737</a></p>
</div>
